AI and Voice Assistant

Effective [EFFECTIVE DATE]

Shop Manager has two features that use artificial intelligence: Bill Scanner, which reads a photographed supplier bill, and the Assistant, which answers questions about the shop’s own records and can be asked out loud. Both are off until the shop is given them and adds a key. This page explains exactly what each one sends, and where.

Who does the AI work

Both features use Google’s Gemini API. Shop Manager sends the request from its server, using a key the shop provides. The key stays on the server and is stored encrypted; it is never sent to the browser.

What is sent to Google

Bill Scanner

The photo of the bill, so the details on it can be read. Nothing else from the shop’s records is sent.

Assistant

Each question is answered in two steps, and Google receives:

  1. the question, and a list of the kinds of lookup the person asking is allowed to run;
  2. the question again, with the records found for it — at most 25 — so the answer is written from them.

Those records can include product names, stock quantities, prices, customer and supplier names, invoice numbers, totals, and amounts owed. They never include phone numbers, PINs, passwords or API keys. A phone number can be searched for, but it is not sent back to Google.

The AI does not reach the database. It can only name one of a fixed list of read-only lookups, and the server decides — against the signed-in person’s own permissions — whether to run it. A staff member who cannot see a figure in the app cannot get it from the Assistant either.

What Google does with it

That depends on the kind of key the shop uses, under Google’s own terms:

  • A key from a Google Cloud project with billing turned on (“paid”): under Google’s published Gemini API terms, what is sent is not used to improve Google’s products, may be held briefly while the request is processed, and is logged for a limited period to detect misuse.
  • A key on the free tier (“unpaid”): under the same terms, Google may use what is sent to improve its products, and people at Google may read it. Google advises against sending personal information to the free tier.

Because the Assistant sends customers’ names and amounts owed, use a key from a project with billing turned on. Shop Manager cannot tell which kind of key it has been given.

Google’s terms change. The current wording is at ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms and governs; this page is a summary and may be out of date.

Whether sending records about a shop’s customers to an AI provider needs a disclosure to, or consent from, those customers — and whether a Data Processing Addendum with Google is needed.

Asking out loud

  • The microphone is used only after someone presses the microphone button, and stops as soon as they stop speaking or press it again.
  • Turning speech into text is done by the web browser’s own speech recognition, not by Shop Manager. Some browsers do this on the device; others send the audio to the browser maker’s servers to do it — Google Chrome, for example, sends it to Google.
  • Shop Manager never records, uploads or keeps audio. What it receives is the text, and that text is handled exactly like a typed question.
  • Answers to spoken questions are read aloud by the browser’s own speech. Some browser voices run on the device; others are online voices that send the answer’s text to the browser maker to be spoken.

What is kept

  • Conversations are not stored. A conversation exists only on the screen it is on, and is gone when the Assistant is closed.
  • Questions and answers are not saved to the shop’s records or written to the server’s logs.
  • Google’s own retention is described above, and is governed by Google’s terms.

What it can and cannot do

The Assistant can only look things up. It cannot create, change or delete anything — no bills, no stock, no customers, no payments. Answers can be wrong: it says where each answer came from, so it can be checked against the record itself.

Each question costs the shop a small amount, charged by Google to the key’s account. To limit runaway cost, a person can ask at most 20 questions a minute.
