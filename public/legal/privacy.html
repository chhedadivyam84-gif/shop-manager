Privacy Policy

Effective [EFFECTIVE DATE] · Operated by [OPERATOR LEGAL NAME]

Shop Manager is billing, stock and accounts software for shops. This policy explains what information the software handles, where it goes, how long it is kept, and who to ask about it. It describes how the software actually works; where something depends on how a particular shop has set it up, it says so.

Who this is about

Two different kinds of information pass through Shop Manager, and they are treated differently:

  • About a business that uses Shop Manager — the shop’s own details, and its staff who sign in. We provide the service to that business.
  • About the people in a business’s records — its customers, suppliers and employees. The business enters this information and decides what it is used for. We hold and process it on the business’s behalf, to provide the software to it.

If you are a customer of a shop that uses Shop Manager, that shop is responsible for your information in its records. Please contact the shop first; see Contact.

Whether the operator acts as a Data Fiduciary or a Data Processor under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 — for each kind of information above — depends on the contracts and the facts, and should be confirmed by a lawyer.

What Shop Manager does not do

  • It contains no advertising and no analytics or tracking of any kind.
  • It does not sell information, or share it with anyone for their own marketing.
  • It uses one cookie, for staying signed in, and no others.
  • It does not record or keep audio.

Cookies

A single session cookie keeps a staff member signed in. It holds a random identifier, not personal details; it cannot be read by scripts on the page; and it lasts up to 30 days, or until the person signs out. It is needed for the software to work at all, so there is no setting to refuse it.

How information is protected

These are measures the software actually takes. No system is perfectly secure, and these are not a guarantee.

  • Each business’s records are kept in a separate database, and a signed-in session can only reach its own business’s.
  • Staff see only the parts of the software the owner has allowed them; this is checked on the server, not just hidden on screen.
  • PINs and passwords are stored only as one-way hashes. API keys are stored encrypted.
  • Hosted copies are served only over HTTPS, with protections against the page being loaded inside other websites or running outside scripts.
  • Repeated wrong PINs are slowed down and locked out.
  • Error logs record what kind of fault happened and where in the software — not the details that were being entered when it happened.
  • Changes are recorded in an activity log that cannot be edited afterwards.
The Information Technology (Reasonable Security Practices and Procedures and Sensitive Personal Data or Information) Rules, 2011 still apply until the relevant parts of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 take effect. Passwords and bank account details are “sensitive personal data” under those Rules. Whether the measures above meet the “reasonable security practices” standard, and whether written consent is needed for collecting that data, should be confirmed by a lawyer.

How long information is kept

Business records are kept while the business uses Shop Manager, and invoices and accounts are kept for as long as GST law requires. The details, and how deletion works, are in the Data Retention and Deletion Policy.

Your choices and rights

If you are in a shop’s records — as a customer, supplier or employee — ask that shop to show, correct or remove your information. The shop’s owner can edit any record. A record linked to past invoices cannot be deleted, because GST law requires those invoices to be kept; it can be corrected or marked inactive instead.

If you are a business or staff member using Shop Manager, contact us using the details on the Contact page to ask what information we hold about you, to correct it, or to ask for it to be removed. We will explain anything we are required by law to keep.

You can complain to our grievance officer, named on the Contact page. Once the relevant provisions of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 are in force, you may also be able to complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

The main obligations under the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 — including notice, consent, security safeguards, breach notification and individuals’ rights — take effect on or around 13 May 2027. The wording of this section should be updated to match them before that date.

Children

Shop Manager is software for businesses and is not meant for children. It does not knowingly collect information from children for its own purposes. A shop’s records may include customers of any age; the shop is responsible for them.
